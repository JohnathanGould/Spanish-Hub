Revenue Model

Four income streams — all compatible with the no-ads, no-subscription promise.

How AdSense is earned — an important distinction

The app itself is 100% ad-free. AdSense revenue is earned through the Milo Speaks Spanish YouTube channel. Each of the 25 Path Stop videos is hosted on YouTube and embedded inside the app as part of the learning experience. When users watch them, those views count toward the channel's watch hours and generate AdSense revenue on the YouTube side. The user never sees an ad. The channel earns passively from their engagement. This is the same model used by educational YouTube channels worldwide.

The four streams

1 · YouTube AdSense

Earned through the YouTube channel — not the app. 25 Stop videos per language. Views accumulate as users progress through Paths. Requires 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours to unlock. Medium-term passive income stream.

2 · Ko-fi (voluntary support)

Completely optional. No features locked behind it. Industry benchmark: 1–3% voluntary conversion. At 1,000 MAU, even 10–15 Ko-fi supporters at $3–6/month covers all infrastructure costs.

3 · Digital workbooks (PDF)

Companion PDF workbooks aligned to each Path. Sold via Gumroad or Ko-fi. $7–12 each, $35–45 bundle. ~95% margin. Zero inventory. Revenue can begin before the app reaches Play Store.

4 · Print-on-demand (Amazon KDP)

Same workbooks on Amazon KDP. No upfront cost, no inventory. ~$4–6 net per unit. A user who finds the workbook on Amazon discovers the free app. Both channels feed each other.

Due Diligence

Every question a COO will ask — answered before she asks it.

🔴 She will almost certainly raise these

1 · Worst case — what do you lose if it fails?She will ask this

A CFO's first move is always downside analysis. What is the total loss if the app never gains traction?

Maximum total cash loss = $580 (including $62 already spent). Skills built are retained permanently. The app remains live. No inventory, no lease, no staff, no debt. The floor is: "we spent $580 and Johnathan learned to build apps with AI."

2 · Time to first dollar — how long before any revenue?She will ask this

The projections show 24 months but don't address when the first revenue unit actually arrives.

Workbooks can go live on Gumroad and Amazon KDP before the app reaches Play Store — potentially within weeks. The first dollar doesn't depend on app store approval, user acquisition, or YouTube thresholds. It depends only on completing a PDF layout.

3 · App Store compliance — won't Apple/Google take 30%?She will ask this

App Store rules require in-app purchases to go through their payment rails (15–30% cut).

Ko-fi donations and PDF workbook purchases happen on external platforms — not inside the app. No app features are locked behind these purchases. This is fully compliant. It is the same model used by Wikipedia, Anki, and hundreds of community-funded apps.

🟡 Sharp COOs often go here

4 · Legal and tax structure in Nova ScotiaWorth preparing

As a sole proprietor in NS, revenue is reported on a personal T1 under self-employment income. HST registration only mandatory above $30,000/year. Business name registration (~$100 in NS) adds professionalism. Worth doing before revenue starts. No incorporation required at this scale.

5 · What stops someone from copying this?Worth preparing

Duolingo cannot credibly promise "no ads, no subscription" — their business model depends on both. The moat is the public commitment, brand trust, the Milo character, and community relationship. None of these can be replicated by a corporation without destroying their own revenue model. Trust is not copyable at scale.

6 · Atlantic Canada grants — has this been explored?Potentially valuable

ACOA (Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency) and NS Digital Economy initiatives exist specifically for this type of project. Worth one Google session before the conversation. Even "I'm looking into NS digital economy grants" shows due diligence and may reduce the personal funding ask.

The People & Animals

A proposal for who belongs in this world — and the business case for including them.

Why this app exists

Milo was a black lab from Truro, Nova Scotia. He wore a plaid tie on trips to the vet and a purple tie at the family's wedding. He lived fourteen years — long enough to become the kind of dog a family builds its identity around. When he passed, his name didn't go with him. It went into an app. A free language learning app, built by his person, that carries his character forward — curious, enthusiastic, always learning, never giving up on a new word.

Every corporate language app has a mascot invented by a marketing team. Milo is real. That distinction is not sentimental — it is a structural competitive advantage that cannot be purchased or replicated.

Part 1 — Milo's friends: the family's other pets

✓ The case for it

Real animals with real personalities. Not invented. Not generic. Genuinely yours. The most authentic cast any language app has ever had.
Pet owners are one of the most loyal and vocal demographics online. Named, real characters earn community word-of-mouth that money cannot buy.
Each character carries their own personality — the nervous cat who hides during drills, the energetic dog who goes wild on a perfect score. Personality is already written.
Extends to the franchise naturally. Milo Speaks French has the same cast in a new setting. The characters travel with the brand.

△ Things to consider

Each character needs art assets and a personality. Manage scope — introduce 2–3 at launch, add others as the app grows.
If a pet passes, their character needs to be handled with care inside the app. Plan for this in advance.
Supporting cast, not competing mascots. Milo is always the lead.
Recommendation: Yes — build this from the start. The pets-as-friends model has no meaningful downside and significant upside in authenticity, community loyalty, and storytelling.

Part 2 — Family as cartoon characters

These would be stylised cartoon representations — friendly illustrated figures, not realistic likenesses. Think Pixar-adjacent. The privacy concern about likeness in a public app is significantly reduced when the format is clearly an illustrated character created with the person's knowledge and blessing.
